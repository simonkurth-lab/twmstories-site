Behind the Curtain

Flex Performing Arts

I came into this as an outsider who knew almost nothing about the dance world.

After several days documenting Flex Performing Arts, I can honestly say I was blown away by the passion, precision and energy I witnessed.

And I'm just talking about the volunteers.

I.

The amount of dedication and effort that goes into pulling off five shows over three days was incredible to see and even more incredible to document.

Of course, what I witnessed was really only the final chapter.

I wasn't privy to the countless hours spent practicing routines, or the logistical challenges of creating elaborate costumes and coordinating recital schedules. The music. The sound. The lighting. The staging. The list goes on and on.

There are so many moving pieces behind a production like this, most of which the audience will never see.

What they do see is what happens when all of those pieces finally come together.

There are moments that happen once, under constantly changing light, and then they're gone. My job was simply to be ready for them.

I felt a tremendous amount of responsibility documenting a production of this scale for the first time.

II.

And the more time I spent around the dancers, the more impressed I became.

The precision, athleticism and passion were impossible to miss.

As a father of two athletes, that athleticism particularly resonated with me. Had dance been part of their world growing up, it's something I would have embraced just as enthusiastically as a dad. There was strength, endurance, timing, coordination and an extraordinary amount of discipline on display.

But athleticism was only part of it.

The performances themselves were characterized by thoughtful and passionate choreography. There were moments of enormous energy and others that were quiet and expressive. Each seemed to carry its own personality and story.

And from the other side of the stage, the pride and support shown by the audience was abundantly clear.

III.

Perhaps that's what stayed with me most after the final performance.

Five shows. Three days. Every one of them different.

Every one of them full of moments that happened only once.

V.

I arrived expecting to document a dance recital.

What I found was an entire community of dancers, teachers, families, volunteers and countless people behind the scenes, collectively working toward the same fleeting moment when the lights come up, the music begins and all that work finally has its few minutes on stage.

Then the lights go down.

I'm grateful I was there to document it.

Thank you to Flex Dance Company and DNA Productions for allowing me into your world and trusting me to document such an important production. It was a privilege to capture it and an experience that left me with a genuine appreciation for everyone who helped bring it to life.

— Simon Kurth, TWM Stories
